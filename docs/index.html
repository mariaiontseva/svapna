Dream technologies in Śaiva-Śākta tantra

Maria Iontseva · DPhil, Oxford

In the tantras a dream is not a sign to be read but a procedure to be performed. Five uses; texts from the 6th to the 19th century; the unedited passages edited from manuscripts.

Introduction What the thesis argues and how.
Argument The dream as a procedure, not a sign.
Claim Śaiva-Śākta tantras treat the dream as a technology: a rite performed on purpose, with a mantra, a prepared bed and a stated result
Five uses to get an answer (Oracle), to test a candidate (Ordeal), to receive a mantra or a scripture (Gift), to act on another person (Weapon), to train consciousness (Discipline)
Not included omens (what it means to see an elephant): the dreamer reads a sign but does nothing
Range 6th c. (Bṛhadyātrā) to 19th c. (Nepalese and Bengali digests)
Method A thematic study with critical editions of unedited passages.
Corpus systematic search of e-texts (Muktabodha and others) by formula, not by title (svapnādhipataye namaḥ, hili hili śūlapāṇaye, svapne kathaya); then catalogues (NCC, NGMPP) for unedited witnesses
Editions local critical editions of the unedited passages from manuscripts, in an appendix
Status for each passage: edited, unedited or catalogue only; unchecked readings and identifications are marked

Svapnamāṇavaka The dream boy, Svapnādhipati: one rite used to ask, to test a candidate, to choose a stone, to start a campaign.
The rite
Rite kuśa bed, one garment, lying on the right side; the dream is reported to the guru in the morning
Verse namaḥ śambho trinetrāya … vāmanāya virūpāya svapnādhipataye namaḥ
Bīja oṃ hili hili śūlapāṇaye svāhā (twelve syllables)
Witnesses at least 27: Śaiva Siddhānta, Kaula, Śākta, Pāñcarātra
Source Varāhamihira, Bṛhadyātrā 16.4–7 (6th c.) → Piṅgalāmata (manuscript of the 1170s) → the tantric witnesses
Recensions of the prayer five: the court verse (bhagavan devadeveśa śūlabhṛd vṛṣavāhana), the Siddhānta form (ācakṣva devadeveśa prapanno ‘smi tavāntikam), the Piṅgalāmata form (svapnaṃ kathaya me śambho), the namo ‘jāya trinetrāya form, and a Vaiṣṇava form (oṃ hṛt sakalalokāya viṣṇave)
Narrative Kathāsaritsāgara 1.6.137–138 (Kashmir, c. 1070): Śarvavarman performs the rite at night and a divine kumāra appears in his dream
South Adyar TR 470-e Svapnaprakāśikā = Bṛhadyātrā 16.4–7 verbatim; copy dated 1689
